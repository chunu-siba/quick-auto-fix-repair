PREVENTATIVE SAFETY

10 WARNING SIGNS WORTH CHECKING

These are general guidance points, not a diagnosis. If a problem feels serious or unsafe, stop driving and seek professional assistance.

High-pitched squealing can be associated with brake-pad wear, while grinding can indicate severe wear. A proper inspection is needed to identify the cause.

A check-engine light can have many causes. A flashing light can indicate a serious active fault, so professional diagnosis should not be delayed.

Pulling to one side can be related to alignment, tires, suspension or braking components. An inspection can identify the actual cause.

Fluid underneath a vehicle can come from several systems. Colour, smell and location can provide clues, but they do not replace an inspection.

Vibration can be associated with wheel balance, tires, suspension or brake components depending on when it occurs.

Slow response can have many causes, including air, fuel, ignition or sensor-related problems. Diagnostics can narrow the possibilities.

Smoke colour and when it appears can provide useful clues, but an inspection is needed before deciding what component has failed.

Unexpected engine revving, delayed engagement or harsh shifts can indicate a transmission or related-system problem requiring diagnosis.

A deep mechanical knock can indicate a serious engine problem. Avoid continuing to drive if the vehicle is behaving dangerously and seek professional advice.

Burning smells can come from oil, coolant, belts, brakes, wiring or other components. The source should be identified rather than guessed.
